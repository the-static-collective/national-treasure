# METHOD

## Purpose

This repository studies unusual, symbolic, archaeological, historical, textual, and architectural mysteries without surrendering either rigor or imagination.

Its function is to preserve an inquiry trail that others can inspect, challenge, extend, and falsify.

## Evidence grammar

Every case should separate five layers.

### 1. Observed
What is directly present?

Examples: a physical feature, wording on a title page, a number visibly counted on an object, a photographed alignment, or an investigator-originated sketch.

### 2. Primary source
What comes from the originating person, artifact, institution, or contemporaneous record?

Examples: Edward Leedskalnin's own pamphlets, site plans, historic records, official architectural descriptions, and photographs.

### 3. Inference
What follows reasonably from observed and primary material?

### 4. Speculation
What might be true but has not yet earned support?

### 5. Failed
What was tested and did not hold up?

Failure is a useful result and should be preserved.

## Operating rules

### Freeze hypotheses before hunting
If a pattern is noticed, record it *before* going looking for confirming instances. This reduces hindsight bias.

### Distinguish symbol overlap from historical transmission
Two sites may share a pattern without one causing the other. Do not convert resemblance into lineage without evidence.

### Prefer counting over vibes
When a hypothesis depends on numbers — 3, 8, 16, 24, 48, 88, etc. — count from the best available source and record the method.

### Pre-register falsifiers
Every hypothesis should include:

- what would increase confidence;
- what would decrease confidence;
- what would falsify it.

### Preserve the weird; preserve the fog
Do not flatten unexplained things into fake certainty. Do not discard a lead merely because it sounds strange. Hold unresolved material in an explicitly unresolved state.

## Confidence ladder

- **C0** — raw thought / not yet tested
- **C1** — grounded speculation
- **C2** — supported by one direct source or verified count
- **C3** — supported by multiple independent sources
- **C4** — robust, repeatable, or strongly evidenced

This is not a truth machine. It tracks how much weight an idea can presently bear.
