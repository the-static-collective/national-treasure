# Sources — Hopi Back Door / Daughter DNA

This source sheet keeps documentary genealogy, Hopi/community interpretation, archaeology, genetics, and oral-memory analogues in separate families.

## A. Back Door documentary genealogy

### A1. Waters, Frank; Fredericks, Oswald White Bear. *Book of the Hopi*. Viking Press, 1963.

**Role:** earliest presently confirmed source in this case carrying the exact English chapter title **“North to the Back Door.”**  
**Provenance:** compilation based on Hopi elder material, mediated through Fredericks and Waters.  
**Quality:** indispensable but contested.  
**Use:** establish the published motif and investigate its source chain; do not treat the English wording as automatically ancient or unmediated.

Stable records:

- Smithsonian Libraries and Archives: https://www.si.edu/object/book-hopi-frank-waters-drawings-and-source-material-recorded-oswald-white-bear-fredericks%3Asiris_sil_771579
- Google Books bibliographic/contents record: https://books.google.com/books/about/Book_of_the_Hopi.html?id=pEN1AAAAMAAJ

### A2. eHRAF World Cultures — *Book of the Hopi* document record.

**Role:** independent anthropological metadata/evaluation of Waters/Fredericks.  
**Provenance:** HRAF analyst summary.  
**Quality:** strong secondary metadata.  
**Important observation:** HRAF states that the material was obtained from thirty Hopi elders and assigns the document its highest source-material rating, “Excellent Primary Data.” This supports taking the source seriously while not resolving later criticism.

https://ehrafworldcultures.yale.edu/cultures/nt09/documents/045

### A3. Geertz, Armin W. “Book of the Hopi: the Hopi's Book?” *Anthropos* 78, no. 3/4 (1983): 547–556.

**Role:** source criticism.  
**Quality:** strong scholarly counterweight to naive use of Waters.  
**Use:** test editing, authority, mediation, and claims that the book straightforwardly represents a unified Hopi voice.

Record: https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/book-of-the-hopi-the-hopi39s-book/

### A4. Geertz, Armin W. *The Invention of Prophecy: Continuity and Meaning in Hopi Indian Religion*.

**Role:** broader study of Hopi narrative tradition, continuity, reinterpretation, and twentieth-century circulation.  
**Quality:** major scholarly source for the transformation problem.  
**Use:** distinguish living narrative tradition from later popular appropriation and understand why continuity need not imply verbatim immobility.

JSTOR record: https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.8306130

## B. Hopi ancestry, clan continuity, and genetics

### B1. Koyiyumptewa, Stewart B.; Colwell, Chip. “Twisting Strings: Hopi Ancestors and Ancient DNA.” In *Critical Perspectives on Ancient DNA*, edited by Daniel Strand, Anna Källén, and Charlotte Mulcare. MIT Press, 2024, pp. 75–96.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/15190.003.0006

**Role:** central source for the “daughter” layer.  
**Provenance:** coauthored by a Hopi community leader/cultural-preservation practitioner and an anthropologist with long Hopi collaboration.  
**Quality:** highest relevance in this case.

The chapter explicitly explains that Hopi clan membership is traced through the female line and explicitly parallels that maternal descent with geneticists' use of maternally inherited mitochondrial DNA. It simultaneously warns that Hopi ancestry is much larger than biological kinship alone.

Open-access book record: https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262548090/critical-perspectives-on-ancient-dna/

### B2. Whiteley, Peter M. “Unpacking Hopi ‘Clans,’ II: Further Questions about Hopi Descent Groups.” *Journal of Anthropological Research* 42, no. 1 (1986).

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1086/jar.42.1.3630380

**Role:** complication/control source for classical descriptions of Hopi descent groups.  
**Use:** prevent “matrilineal” from being flattened into a complete account of Hopi social structure.

### B3. Colwell, Chip; Koyiyumptewa, Stewart B. “Traditional Cultural Properties and the Hopi Model of Cultural Preservation.” In *Footprints of Hopi History*.

**Role:** community-centered methodological context.  
**Use:** establishes the Hopi Cultural Preservation Office's collaborative/community-based model and why research touching ancestry should remain relevant, respectful, and beneficial to Hopi people.

University of Arizona Press open-access volume: https://open.uapress.arizona.edu/

## C. Hopi migration traditions and archaeology

### C1. Bernardini, Wesley. “Understanding Ancestral Hopi Migration and Identity, A.D. 1275–1400.” *Archaeology Southwest* 21, no. 2.

**Role:** key archaeological control hypothesis.  
**Quality:** strong synthesis connecting Hopi migration traditions with archaeological patterning while refusing simplistic one-route mapping.

Bernardini emphasizes that Hopi clan migration traditions are complex, nonlinear, and composed from the histories of smaller social units; in some respects those traditions fit archaeological evidence better than older archaeologists' fixed “culture area” models.

PDF archive: https://www.archaeologysouthwest.org/pdf/arch-sw-v21-no2.pdf

### C2. *Footprints of Hopi History: Hopihiniwtiput Kukveni'at*.

**Role:** broader collaborative volume on Hopi history, migration, archaeology, landscape, and cultural preservation.  
**Use:** locate migration traditions inside community-centered historical work rather than using Waters as the only doorway.

University of Arizona Press open-access materials: https://open.uapress.arizona.edu/

## D. Beringia and Indigenous American population history

### D1. Tamm, Erika, et al. “Beringian Standstill and Spread of Native American Founders.” *PLoS ONE* 2, no. 9 (2007): e829.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0000829

**Role:** classic mtDNA source for Beringian isolation and later rapid spread.  
**Use:** supports the claim that founder maternal lineages differentiated around a Beringian source population rather than merely passing unchanged through a one-day doorway.

### D2. Perego, Ugo A., et al. “The Phylogeny of the Four Pan-American mtDNA Haplogroups: Implications for Evolutionary and Disease Studies.” *PLoS ONE* 3, no. 3 (2008): e1764.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0001764

**Role:** high-resolution mtDNA phylogeny of major pan-American haplogroups.  
**Use:** founder-lineage branching and continental radiation; historical age estimates should be read in light of later calibration and ancient-DNA work.

### D3. Llamas, Bastien, et al. “Ancient Mitochondrial DNA Provides High-Resolution Time Scale of the Peopling of the Americas.” *Science Advances* 2, no. 4 (2016): e1501385.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1501385

**Role:** ancient-mitogenome chronology.  
**Use:** supports eastern-Beringian isolation followed by expansion around ~16 ka in the authors' model.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27051878/

### D4. Mulligan, Connie J. “The Peopling of the Americas and the Origin of the Beringian Occupation Model.” *American Journal of Physical Anthropology* 162, no. 3 (2017): 403–408.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.23152

**Role:** concise history/synthesis of the Beringian occupation model.  
**Use:** separates divergence from Asian ancestors, Beringian isolation/diversification, and rapid continental expansion.

### D5. Moreno-Mayar, J. Víctor, et al. “Terminal Pleistocene Alaskan Genome Reveals First Founding Population of Native Americans.” *Nature* 553 (2018): 203–207.

DOI / article: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature25173

**Role:** Ancient Beringian genome from Upward Sun River.  
**Use:** nuclear-genome evidence for deep branching among populations ancestral/related to Native Americans in Beringia.

### D6. Castro e Silva, Marcos Araújo, et al. “The Evolutionary History and Unique Genetic Diversity of Indigenous Americans.” *Nature* (2026).

Article: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10406-w

**Role:** current broad genomic synthesis and new high-coverage Indigenous American genomes.  
**Use:** current statement that Indigenous American ancestry formation began about 25,000 years ago in and around Beringia, followed after partial isolation by major dispersal and later complex continental differentiation.

**Restraint:** this broad continental result does not identify the ancestry or route encoded by any particular Hopi narrative.

### D7. Malhi, Ripan S., et al. “Native American mtDNA Prehistory in the American Southwest.” *American Journal of Physical Anthropology* 120, no. 2 (2003): 108–124.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.10138

**Role:** regional population-genetic context.  
**Use:** demonstrate that Southwest mtDNA has been studied in relation to migration hypotheses.

**Restraint:** legacy regional sampling cannot bear a story-specific Pleistocene inference by itself.

## E. Deep oral-memory possibility controls

### E1. Nunn, Patrick D.; Reid, Nicholas J. “Aboriginal Memories of Inundation of the Australian Coast Dating from More than 7000 Years Ago.” *Australian Geographer* 47, no. 1 (2016).

**Role:** widely discussed attempt to test very long-lived oral memory against independently dated sea-level change.  
**Use:** possibility proof that some environmental oral traditions may persist for millennia.

**Restraint:** methodology and inferred ages are debated. Even if accepted, this gives no direct support to a Hopi Beringia claim.

### E2. Long-memory literature generally

**Role:** methodological control.  
**Use:** evaluate what kinds of details survive, how narratives transform, and which independent chronological anchors are required before assigning deep antiquity.

The case should actively seek skeptical literature as well as positive analogues.

## F. Highest-value next retrievals

1. Locate a scan or physical copy of *Book of the Hopi*, pp. 37–46, and record the exact Back Door wording with page-level citation.
2. Locate Fredericks' surviving tape recordings, transcripts, field notes, or archival papers, if preserved, and compare them against Waters' published chapter.
3. Search pre-1963 Hopi ethnographies, especially Fewkes, Voth, Stephen, Titiev, Eggan, and community-preserved records, for equivalent northern-boundary motifs **without using “Back Door” as the only search term**.
4. Identify the Hopi-language term rendered by Waters/Fredericks as “Back Door,” if recoverable, and obtain a language-competent semantic analysis.
5. Search Hopi Cultural Preservation Office publications for public/community-approved discussions of northward clan migration and emergence geography.
6. Build a motif table only after independent variants are found: direction, boundary, ice/snow, water, mountains, peoples beyond, route order, ritual function, clan attribution, and stopping places.
7. Keep the genetic inquiry continental unless Hopi-led research supplies ethically appropriate finer-grained evidence.

## Source rule for this case

No number of later websites repeating Waters counts as independent evidence.

A motif earns historical weight only when its **source ancestry** becomes clearer than its resemblance.
