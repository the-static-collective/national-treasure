## Failure ledger

### F-001 — resemblance as transmission

**Rejected formulation:** `north + ice + boundary = Beringia memory proved`.

Why it fails: those features are too general by themselves. A transmission claim needs source genealogy, chronology, diagnostic details, and alternatives tested against the same evidence.

### F-002 — genetics as translation key

**Rejected formulation:** `daughter means mitochondrial DNA in the Hopi narrative`.

Why it fails: the mtDNA parallel is structurally real and explicitly noticed by Koyiyumptewa and Colwell, but no evidence presently identifies it as the intended semantic content of the Back Door narrative.

### F-003 — one-date land-bridge cartoon

**Rejected formulation:** `the ancestors crossed Beringia at ~15 ka, full stop`.

Why it fails: present genetic models separate ancestry formation, partial isolation, branching, dispersal, and later continental differentiation across a longer Late Pleistocene interval.

### F-004 — genetic ancestry as sovereign ancestry

**Rejected formulation:** `genomic affinity decides Hopi identity or historical authority`.

Why it fails: genetic relation is only one strand. Hopi concepts of ancestry and obligation exceed a biological pedigree, and ethical interpretation requires community partnership rather than genomic capture.

### F-005 — mnemonic tree as literal phylogeny

**Rejected formulation:** `ancestral C1 → C1a (Asia) versus one American C1 node → C1b/C1c/C1d` as though that were the demonstrated branch structure.

Why it fails: the simplified drawing is useful for intuition but not safe as a literal phylogeny. High-resolution studies resolve multiple related C1 branches and multiple founder genomes; the actual topology should be cited from the relevant phylogenetic analysis.
