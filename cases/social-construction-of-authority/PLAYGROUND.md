# Playground — historical mechanism experiments

This is a **research worksheet** for playing with hypotheses without silently converting them into facts. Use [SOURCES](./SOURCES.md), [CLAIM-LEDGER](./CLAIM-LEDGER.md) and [MECHANISM-ATLAS](./MECHANISM-ATLAS.md) beside it.

## How to run a round

For each experiment, write **one narrow claim**, attach an independently findable source, trace only witnessed arrows, invent the strongest plausible rival explanation, and state what would make you change your mind. Keep two labels for each claim: `provenance_class` and `support_confidence` from [METHOD](../../METHOD.md). A blank stage is not permission to auto-complete it.

### Experiment A — The old book inside the new stone

**Specimen:** Shabaka Stone, British Museum EA498 (c. 710 BCE).

1. Record the surviving object's date. In a separate field record the earlier date *claimed by* the inscription.
2. Ask which material evidence, if any, identifies the purported original manuscript.
3. Compare two hypotheses: (H1) an older tradition was preserved; (H2) presentation as an ancient recovery was a contemporary royal literary device. These can also overlap.
4. Look for independent reception evidence before inferring political success.
5. **Counterfactual:** if the rescue narrative were absent but the Ptah theology unchanged, what conclusions about the monument's self-presentation would still hold?

**Expected boundary:** `stone attested` and `preservation claim attested` do **not** entail `claimed source date verified`.

### Experiment B — Is the seal sufficient?

**Specimen:** Met 327283 and historically documented Mesopotamian sealings; no modern credential attack or impersonation.

Build two *hypothetical* records, labeling them invented:
- **B1:** a seal impression survives on a tablet, but no independent witness for who rolled it.
- **B2:** a seal impression survives together with a separate contemporaneous witness establishing issuer, bounded mandate and documented transaction.

For each mark `SIGN_VISIBLE`, `ISSUER_ATTESTED`, `AUTHORITY_IN_CONTEXT`, `ACTION_DOCUMENTED`, `OUTCOME_DOCUMENTED` as **yes / no / unknown**. Unknown is not no. Is one specimen enough to establish more than the other? Which further evidence would matter?

**Counterfactual:** duplicate an appearance without a valid issuer. What breaks? Do not turn this into practical instructions for impersonation.

### Experiment C — Same decree, different audiences

**Specimen:** Rosetta Stone, British Museum EA24 (196 BCE).

Create three audience lanes labeled **hieroglyphic / Demotic / Greek**. Enter only established information about the scripts, the priestly issuer, the decree and attested copies. Leave actual audience-level recognition and behavior **unknown** unless independently attested.

Now vary one analytic assumption at a time:
- identical institutional issuance, different linguistic carrier;
- a copy exists but its audience did not encounter it;
- recipients encountered it but did not accept the institution's asserted status.

Which claims about dissemination still hold in each scenario, and which claims about reception no longer follow? *These are logical counterfactuals, not findings about actual ancient audiences.*

### Experiment D — Text reused; office not automatically transferred

**Specimen:** Isaiah 22:15–25 and Revelation 3:7–12.

Trace separately: `shared wording`, `documented intertext`, `textual reinterpretation`, `actual institutional delegation`. The first three may be supportable from the texts; the last needs independent institutional evidence.

**Adversarial challenge:** replace the distinctive David/key/open/shut wording with a generic image of a door. Does the proposed textual ancestry remain identifiable, or does the evidence become too weak?

### Experiment E — One symbol, zero automatic power

Use an **invented fictional community**, explicitly not a reconstruction of history:
- A circle is painted on a wall; nobody recognizes it.
- A group recognizes it as its gathering-place marker.
- The group develops an agreed rule for using the marker.
- A rival group adopts a visually identical circle independently.

What different facts are present at each stage? Which actions could be evidenced? Does the rival symbol inherit any authority simply because it has the same shape? Build a negative specimen in which a group openly refuses the marker.
