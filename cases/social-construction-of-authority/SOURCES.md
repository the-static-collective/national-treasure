# Source roads — Social Construction of Authority

Each source below has a **bounded use**. An artifact documents its own language and material features, not automatically its author's accuracy, readers' beliefs, causal effect, or connection to another artifact. Source dates refer to surviving specimens or composition contexts where known.

## Primary / collection records

1. **Shabaka Stone — British Museum EA498.** Twenty-fifth Dynasty monument, catalogued c. 710 BCE, found at Memphis. Its inscription *claims* a royal act of recovering a damaged old writing; the British Museum notes the possibility of ancient fiction and debates about when the composition originated. It transmits the Memphite creation account of Ptah. https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA498
   - **Can support:** content and self-presentation of this surviving monument; stated attribution to Shabaka; religious subject.
   - **Cannot independently support:** an actual lost manuscript of the asserted age; every detail of intended political strategy; uniform reception by the population.

2. **Rosetta Stone — British Museum EA24**, 196 BCE, decree of Egyptian priests concerning Ptolemy V in hieroglyphic, Demotic and Greek. https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA24 ; contextual discussion: https://www.britishmuseum.org/blog/everything-you-ever-wanted-know-about-rosetta-stone
   - **Can support:** attested multilingual official decree and institutional message; extant related copies.
   - **Cannot independently support:** identical nuance in each linguistic form, universal readership or measured persuasion of every community.

3. **Old Babylonian cylinder seal — Metropolitan Museum of Art 327283**, c. eighteenth–seventeenth centuries BCE. Museum description explains use of cylinder impressions on clay sealing jars, doors, baskets and on commercial/legal tablets. https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/327283
   - **Can support:** attested identity/ownership and administrative uses of the artifact class.
   - **Cannot independently support:** validity of a specific unseen transaction; the subjective conviction of each person encountering an impression.

4. **Behistun/Bisotun — UNESCO World Heritage**, Darius I's royal relief and trilingual monumental inscription dating to his accession conflicts c. 521–520 BCE. https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1222
   - **Can support:** the king's documented monumental presentation and languages, depicted and claimed events.
   - **Cannot independently support:** every claim about rival rulers or the political effect of the monument on all audiences.

5. **Isaiah 22:15–25 and Revelation 3:7–12.** Use the text and comparison already preserved in [Key of David SOURCES](../key-of-david/SOURCES.md), including Hebrew, Old Greek Isaiah and Greek Revelation.
   - **Can support:** office transfer/key/open/shut context; Revelation's subsequent reuse of the wording.
   - **Cannot independently support:** universal historical "key technology," an unbroken institutional chain, or an occult function of all keys.

6. **Matthew 1:1–17.** Primary text of the three groups of fourteen in the genealogy; see the [Key of David claim ledger](../key-of-david/CLAIM-LEDGER.md), NT-KOD-007.
   - **Can support:** deliberate literary arrangement of the genealogy.
   - **Cannot independently support:** a method of population control, or every inference from numeric correspondences.

## Secondary framework / conceptual controls

7. **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Collective Intentionality."** Defines shared intention, collective acceptance, joint action; distinguishes these from a mere coincidence of individual intentions. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/collective-intentionality/
8. **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Social Institutions."** Reviews competing theories of institutional status, deontic power, collective acceptance, and goals; use as a map of disagreements, not a final proof of any one theory. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/social-institutions/
