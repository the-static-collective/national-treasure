# The Social Construction of Authority — a National Treasure case

**Status:** active *research scaffold* on a case-isolated branch; becomes a landed case only if merged to `main`.
**Opened:** 2026-09-23.
**Lead:** National Treasure's historical/provenance method. **Stimulus:** an investigation of Sevan Bomar's 2009 *The Code to the Matrix* and its claims about words, symbols, names, numbers, and authority. The book is a modern comparative prompt, not independent evidence of ancient history.

## Question

**Under which evidenced circumstances does a representation of authority become recognized and consequential in human social life?**

A sign, name, ritual, seal, ancient-history claim, translation, official account, or administrative category may be involved in social coordination. This case examines *particular attested mechanisms*, not an assumed universal secret code, a unified historical controller, or a claim that all social organization is manipulation.

"Social engineering" has different senses: cooperative institution design, deliberate influence, and deceptive impersonation. Do not collapse these into one moral category or infer deception from the mere existence of a shared symbol.

## First working model (hypothesis, not an empirical law)

```text
representation / claim
        ↓  who issued it, when, under what warrant?
attributable issuance
        ↓  who recognized it, and in what bounded context?
recognition
        ↓  what actions can actually be observed?
documented exercise
        ↓  whose conditions materially changed?
consequence
        ↓  could recognition be contested, withdrawn, or corrected?
review / refusal / revision
```

**No automatic promotion:** an object may *claim* ancient origin without being ancient; a ruler may *claim* legitimacy without universal recognition; people may recognize a sign without taking an attributable action; observed action may not prove the sign caused it. A later interpretive label cannot make an earlier object part of its proposed lineage.

## Case-specific connections, not merged histories

- [Key of David](../key-of-david/README.md): Isaiah 22's royal household key signifies delegated access authority; Revelation 3 reuses it. Later reception is a documented textual relation, not proof that every key or credential descends from this imagery.
- [Palimpsest stack](../palimpsest-stack/README.md): form, name, meaning, ancestry, and reception can change separately. Similarity alone cannot prove transmission.
- [Trust archaeology](../trust-archaeology/README.md): compare historical authority claims with bounded, reviewable custodianship; do not import a research analogy as legal validity.
- Shabaka Stone: a monument *claims* preservation of an earlier damaged text. The age of the surviving monument and that claim are different facts.
- Rosetta Stone: a priestly decree concerning Ptolemy V survives in three writing systems; the inscription and its institutional setting are real, while its impact on individual beliefs requires separate evidence.
- Mesopotamian seals: material symbols used in identification, sealing and documentation; an impression is not a universal proof that its apparent owner authorized a particular event.
- Behistun: Darius's monumental account is primary evidence for how Darius publicly represented his accession and conflicts, not neutral certification that his account is entirely true.

## Immediate use

Start with **PLAYGROUND Experiment A** to separate a *claimed* old text from a demonstrably old artifact. Continue with **Experiment B** to test whether a symbol's appearance alone yields any authority. For a new find, record date, original language, carrier, named issuer, reception, observable conduct, material consequence, competing explanation, and an adverse/negative specimen.

**Promotion boundary:** no broad claim of a society-wide "code" or deliberate psychological operation without separately evidenced actors, intent, transmission, reception, and effect. The method in [../../METHOD.md](../../METHOD.md) governs this case.
