In Caroline Waerzeggers and Melanie M. Groß (eds.), *Personal Names in Cuneiform Texts from Babylonia (c. 750–100 BCE): An Introduction*, Cambridge University Press, 2024, pp. 139–165.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009291071.011
- Cambridge chapter: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/personal-names-in-cuneiform-texts-from-babylonia-c-750100-bce/hebrew-names/D311D2597B53E929381C5AB380DCA2DE
- Open PDF: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/D311D2597B53E929381C5AB380DCA2DE/9781009291088c9_139-165.pdf/hebrew_names.pdf

Relevant points to verify in the source:

- cuneiform renderings of the Yahwistic element such as `Yāḫû-`;
- same-tablet cuneiform/alphabetic correspondences such as Yāḫû-natan / `yhwntn`;
- the warning that the original Hebrew articulation of YHWH is not directly known;
- Babylonian scribes' renderings are informative but constrained by the cuneiform syllabary;
- the Judean name written `ia-ḫu-ú-šú-ú`, normalized approximately `Yāḫû-šūʿ`.

- https://www.biblindex.org/en/bible/septuaginta/is-22%3A22

```text
καὶ δώσω τὴν δόξαν Δαυιδ αὐτῷ,
καὶ ἄρξει,
```


## J. Negative / boundary receipts

These absences and cautions are part of the case:

- No ancient receipt has been found that Jesus's brothers called him **Yashi**.
- `Yashi ~ Yishai/Jesse` is resemblance, not ancestry.
- `דוד = door-hinge-door` is not accepted historical etymology.
- waw/vav is more closely associated historically with hook/peg than “hinge”; hinge is a modern relational metaphor.
- `Yāḫû-šūʿ` does not automatically equal the exact spoken name of Jesus of Nazareth.
- Revelation's Hebrew-aligned Isaiah wording does not by itself prove direct personal translation from Hebrew.
- `peg -> pillar` remains an unconfirmed literary-inversion hypothesis.
- sevenfold Spirit imagery does not by itself prove one intentionally encoded menorah system.
