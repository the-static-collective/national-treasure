# Tarot continuity — source ledger

Accessed 2026-08-24 unless otherwise stated. Sources are grouped by evidentiary role, not prestige. Museum object records are strong for the object they catalog; interpretive essays are independently checked.

## A. Card substrate and early Tarot

### S01 — International Playing-Card Society

- [A brief history of playing cards](https://i-p-c-s.org/history.html)
- [An introduction to Tarot](https://www.i-p-c-s.org/wp/an-introduction/)

Use: specialist overview for 1377 European records, early Italian Tarot, pack structure, terminology, regional games, and the late occult fork. Limit: synthesis rather than a substitute for each primary record.

### S02 — Metropolitan Museum of Art, “The World in Play: Tarot”

- [Tarot](https://www.metmuseum.org/perspectives/tarot-2)

Use: concise museum synthesis of Italian origins, game structure, early courts, and surviving packs. Checked against object records and specialist literature.

### S03 — Los Angeles County Museum of Art, Mamluk cards

- [Playing Card Fragment](https://collections.lacma.org/object/114977)

Use: object-level evidence for an Egyptian four-suit pack with cups, coins, swords, and polo sticks.

### S04 — Andrew Lo, Chinese card source criticism

- [“The Game of Leaves: An Inquiry into the Origin of Chinese Playing Cards,” SOAS record](https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/output/447249/the-game-of-leaves-an-inquiry-into-the-origin-of-chinese-playing-cards)
- [Cambridge abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-school-of-oriental-and-african-studies/article/abs/game-of-leaves-an-inquiry-into-the-origin-of-chinese-playing-cards/FCDF32E1461700E7617AAD4AA1750D56)
- [David Parlett’s accessible summary](https://www.parlettgames.uk/histocs/leafgame.html)

Use: rejection of the Tang “leaf game” shortcut and support for a 1294 documentary floor. Limit: does not complete a transmission genealogy to Europe.

### S05 — Franco Pratesi, Florentine evidence

- [“The Earliest Tarot Pack Known,” PDF](https://www.naibi.net/p/73-TARSOURC-Z.pdf)

Use: Giusto Giusti’s copied 1440 entry, Florentine accounts, and the multicenter origin problem. Limit: origin proposals remain interpretations of incomplete evidence.

### S06 — Morgan Library & Museum, Visconti–Sforza cards

- [Visconti–Sforza Tarot Cards, collection viewer](https://ica.themorgan.org/manuscript/page/14/158525)

Use: high-resolution object evidence for a mid-fifteenth-century Italian painted pack.

### S07 — Metropolitan Museum of Art exhibition catalog and galleries

### S08 — British Museum, Sola Busca

## B. Cartomancy and occult reinterpretation

### S10 — Antoine Court de Gébelin, 1781

- [*Monde primitif*, volume VIII](https://archive.org/details/mondeprimitifana08cour)

Use: primary evidence for the influential Egyptian-origin conjecture.

### S11 — Etteilla, 1770

- [*Etteilla, ou l’art de lire dans les cartes*](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k3250717)

Use: primary evidence for printed ordinary-card cartomancy before Court’s Tarot chapter.

### S12 — Arthur Edward Waite

- [*The Pictorial Key to the Tarot*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Pictorial_Key_to_the_Tarot)

Use: primary account of Waite’s system and his rejection of a literal Egyptian history. It represents Waite’s program, not Smith’s entire visual authorship.

## E. Source cautions

- A source can be excellent for an object and weak for a sweeping ancestry claim.
- Public-domain primary texts show what an author asserted, not that the assertion was true.
- Surviving luxury decks are not a representative sample of all early play.
- “No evidence” means a positive claim has not met its burden; it does not prove metaphysical impossibility.
- Current-use surveys and interview studies cannot be projected across eras or countries.
- Every “first” in this case is qualified by category: documented, surviving, complete, printed, mass-published, scenic, or purpose-designed.
