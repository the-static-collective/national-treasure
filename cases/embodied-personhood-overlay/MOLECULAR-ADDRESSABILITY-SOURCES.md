# Molecular Addressability — Source Ledger

**Status:** supporting ledger for `MOLECULAR-ADDRESSABILITY.md`  
**Date added:** 2026-08-20

This file preserves the evidence floor for the sixth layer of the Embodied Personhood Overlay. It is intentionally narrower than a full pharmacology bibliography.

The governing source rule is:

> **Mechanistic examples may illustrate molecular addressability, but one famous target must not be mistaken for a compound's complete pharmacology.**

---

## A. Endocannabinoid system

### Kano, M. (2014) — endocannabinoid retrograde signaling

**Title:** Control of synaptic function by endocannabinoid-mediated retrograde signaling  
**PMID:** 25169670  
**DOI:** 10.2183/pjab.90.235  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25169670/

Useful for:

- 2-AG as a major endocannabinoid;
- postsynaptic production and retrograde action at presynaptic CB1 receptors;
- suppression/modulation of neurotransmitter release;
- distinction between 2-AG and anandamide signaling roles.

### Zou & Kumar (2018) — cannabinoid receptors and central signaling

**Title:** Cannabinoid Receptors and the Endocannabinoid System: Signaling and Function in the Central Nervous System  
**PMID:** 29533978  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29533978/

Useful for:

- CB1/CB2 signaling architecture;
- AEA and 2-AG;
- simplified retrograde synaptic model;
- presynaptic CB1-mediated effects.

### Maddox et al. (2026) — FAAH/MAGL modulation

**Title:** FAAH and MAGL inhibition: Evolving approaches to treating substance use disorders  
**PMID:** 41350173  
**PMCID:** PMC12976495  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.neurot.2025.e00814  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41350173/

Useful for:

- FAAH and MAGL as endocannabinoid-metabolizing enzymes;
- indirect modulation of CB1 signaling by altering endogenous ligand metabolism;
- evidence that molecular control can occur at degradation machinery rather than only at receptors.

### Older overview retained for architecture

**Title:** Endocannabinoid system: An overview of its potential in current medical practice  
**PMID:** 19675519  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19675519/

Useful for the classical simplified ECS picture: AEA, 2-AG, CB1, CB2, FAAH, MAGL. Treat as historical overview rather than the final word on transporter or receptor complexity.

## B. Endogenous opioid system

### Corder et al. (2018)

**Title:** Endogenous and Exogenous Opioids in Pain  
**PMID:** 29852083  
**PMCID:** PMC6428583  
**DOI:** 10.1146/annurev-neuro-080317-061522  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29852083/

Useful for:

- endogenous opioid receptors and peptide families;
- μ, δ, κ and nociceptin receptor systems;
- β-endorphin, enkephalins, dynorphins and nociceptin/orphanin FQ;
- the important evolutionary inversion: exogenous opioids act on an endogenous regulatory system.

## C. Caffeine / adenosine

### Fredholm, Yang & Wang (2017)

**Title:** Low, but not high, dose caffeine is a readily available probe for adenosine actions  
**PMID:** 27915051  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.mam.2016.11.011  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27915051/

Useful for:

- low-dose caffeine as an antagonist at A1, A2A and A2B adenosine receptors;
- dose-dependent mechanistic caution;
- the distinction between adding an “energy signal” and blocking part of an endogenous inhibitory/regulatory signal.

## D. Benzodiazepines / allosteric modulation

### Jiang et al. (2026)

**Title:** The Evolution of Benzodiazepine Allosteric Modulators: Structural Insights From Historical Milestones to Emerging Innovations in Drug Discovery and Development  
**PMID:** 41208204  
**DOI:** 10.1002/med.70026  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41208204/

Useful for:

- the “receptor as control panel” rather than single lock-and-key metaphor.

**Title:** Structural determinants of benzodiazepine allosteric regulation of GABA(A) receptor currents  
**PMID:** 16135763

## E. Receptor promiscuity / evolutionary specificity

### Eick et al. (2012)

**Title:** Evolution of minimal specificity and promiscuity in steroid hormone receptors  
**PMID:** 23166518  
**PMCID:** PMC3499368  
**DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pgen.1003072  
**Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23166518/

Useful for:

- evolutionary receptor promiscuity;
- “good enough” rather than perfect ligand discrimination;
- sensitivity to endogenous and xenobiotic ligands;
- a concrete evolutionary mechanism supporting the claim that exogenous molecules can affect receptors without those receptors having evolved specifically for those compounds.

This source should **not** be generalized to every receptor family without additional evidence. It is a strong specimen, not a universal proof.

## F. Explicit non-equations

```text
THC activates CB1
    ≠
CB1 evolved for cannabis

morphine activates opioid receptors
    ≠
opioid receptors evolved for poppies

compound alters neurotransmission
    ≠
compound contains the resulting experience

endogenous
    ≠
healthy / safe / morally good

exogenous
    ≠
toxic / unnatural / morally bad

one famous receptor target
    ≠
complete pharmacology

plant-human molecular fit
    ≠
spiritual or historical correspondence demonstrated
```
