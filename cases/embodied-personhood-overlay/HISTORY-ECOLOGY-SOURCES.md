## A. Adaptive immune memory

### Lam N, Lee Y, Farber DL. *A guide to adaptive immune memory.* Nature Reviews Immunology. 2024.

- PMID: **38831162**
- DOI: `10.1038/s41577-024-01040-6`
- Role in case: high-authority review of memory T cells, B cells, plasma cells, antibodies, faster/more effective responses after antigen re-exposure, and vaccination logic.
- Supports:
  - biological memory is not exclusively neural;
  - prior antigen encounter can alter later response architecture.
- Confidence: **Established**

### Sadeghi M, Divangahi M. *Discovering adaptive features of innate immune memory.* Immunological Reviews. 2024.

- PMID: **38563500**
- DOI: `10.1111/imr.13328`
- Role in case: source for memory-like adaptation in innate immunity / trained-immunity literature.
- Restraint: do not collapse adaptive immune memory and trained innate immunity into one mechanism.
- Confidence: **Established as research field; mechanism/context specific**

## B. Cannabinoid tolerance as molecular history

### *Mechanisms of cannabinoid tolerance.* 2023 review.

- PMID: **37348821**
- Role in case: CB1 receptor desensitization, downregulation, intracellular signaling, receptor trafficking, and tolerance mechanisms.
- Supports:
  - repeated ligand exposure can alter the later state of the receiving system;
  - molecular addressability is history-dependent.
- Restraint: human and animal evidence differ; not all tolerance is reducible to CB1 abundance.
- Confidence: **Established / mechanistically heterogeneous**

### *Role of receptor internalization in the agonist-induced desensitization of cannabinoid type 1 receptors.* 2008.

- PMID: **17986216**
- DOI: `10.1111/j.1471-4159.2007.05063.x`
- Role in case: direct mechanistic specimen of CB1 internalization, recycling, desensitization, and resensitization.
- Restraint: cell/culture and experimental agonist findings are not direct whole-person claims.
- Confidence: **Established mechanistic specimen**

## C. Epigenetic persistence / inheritance boundary

### Khatib H et al. *Calling the question: what is mammalian transgenerational epigenetic inheritance?* Epigenetics. 2024.

- PMID: **38525788**
- PMCID: **PMC10965103**
- DOI: `10.1080/15592294.2024.2333586`
- Role in case: strong caution source. Notes robust evidence in some non-mammalian systems while emphasizing that mammalian TEI remains controversial because of germline/embryonic reprogramming and confounds.
- Supports:
  - epigenetic regulation is real;
  - transgenerational inheritance is a separate, harder claim.
- Confidence: **Established boundary / unresolved stronger claim**

### Moelling K. *Epigenetics and transgenerational inheritance.* Journal of Physiology. 2024.

- PMID: **37772441**
- DOI: `10.1113/JP284424`
- Role in case: general epigenetic regulation and environmental influence review.
- Restraint: use alongside more skeptical/methodological sources; do not use as sole authority for human TEI.

### *Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance: an open discussion.* 2015.

- PMID: **26344807**
- Role in case: methodological caution about definitional precision and overtranslation from animal models to humans.
- Confidence: **Useful adversarial source**

Standing distinction:

```text
somatic epigenetic regulation
        ≠
developmental programming
        ≠
intergenerational exposure effect
        ≠
true transgenerational inheritance
```

## D. Microbiota–gut–brain axis

### Lee S-H, Han C, Shin C. *IUPHAR review: Microbiota-gut-brain axis and its role in neuropsychiatric disorders.* Pharmacological Research. 2025.

- PMID: **40306604**
- DOI: `10.1016/j.phrs.2025.107749`
- Role in case: recent pharmacology review describing bidirectional neuronal, endocrine, immunological, and chemical communication between gut microbiota and brain.
- Supports:
  - gut–brain interaction is multi-channel rather than one cable;
  - microbial metabolites and host systems interact.
- Restraint: disorder associations do not automatically establish microbial causation.
- Confidence: **Established axis / variable disease causality**

### Park JC et al. *Beyond the gut: decoding the gut-immune-brain axis in health and disease.* Cellular & Molecular Immunology. 2025.

- PMID: **40804450**
- Role in case: gut–immune–brain integration; microbial metabolites, barriers, immune signaling, neuronal pathways.
- Key caution preserved from review: much mechanistic insight remains preclinical.
- Confidence: **Established mechanistic framework; human translation variable**

### *The microbiota-gut-brain axis and central nervous system diseases: from mechanisms of pathogenesis to therapeutic strategies.* 2025.

- PMID: **40584038**
- PMCID: **PMC12202378**
- DOI: `10.3389/fmicb.2025.1583562`
- Role in case: broad review of immune, metabolic, neuroactive, and barrier-related routes.
- Restraint: broad narrative review; use for mechanism map, not treatment certainty.

### *The gut-brain axis: role of gut microbiota in neurological disease pathogenesis and pharmacotherapeutics.* 2025.

- PMID: **41186720**
- DOI: `10.1007/s00210-025-04776-7`
- Role in case: neural, endocrine, immunological, metabolic route synthesis.
- Restraint: disease linkage is heterogeneous and often associative.

## E. Microbial metabolites as molecular-addressability bridge

### Interino N et al. *Microbiota-Gut-Brain Axis: Mass-Spectrometry-Based Metabolomics in the Study of Microbiome Mediators-Stress Relationship.* Biomolecules. 2025.

- PMID: **40001546**
- DOI: `10.3390/biom15020243`
- Role in case: microbial and host metabolites as measurable mediators in the gut–brain axis.
- Supports:
  - ecology can alter the host chemical environment;
  - microbial metabolism can feed into host molecular control surfaces.

### *Microbial metabolites in the gut-brain axis: their impact on depression pathophysiology and treatment.* 2025/2026 indexing.

- PMID: **41344619**
- DOI: `10.1016/j.neuroscience.2025.12.002`
- Role in case: tryptophan products, SCFAs, GABA-related and other microbial metabolites; explicit cross-pathway synthesis.
- Important restraint: review emphasizes mixed animal/human evidence and translational limitations.

### *Gut bacteria-derived metabolites and their implications in mental health and neurological diseases.* 2025.

- PMID: **41160236**
- Role in case: SCFAs, tryptophan derivatives, bile acids, neuroimmune and barrier pathways.
- Restraint: mechanistic plausibility ≠ clinical causality.

## F. Evidence boundaries for the ecological-self layer

The following propositions must remain separate:

```text
microbes live in/on humans
        ↓
ESTABLISHED

microbial metabolites affect host pathways
        ↓
ESTABLISHED in many specific mechanisms

microbiome composition associates with disease
        ↓
COMMONLY OBSERVED

microbiome difference causes disease
        ↓
CASE-SPECIFIC / OFTEN UNRESOLVED

microbiome intervention treats disease
        ↓
INTERVENTION-SPECIFIC / REQUIRES TRIAL EVIDENCE

microbiome determines personality or personhood
        ↓
UNSUPPORTED AS GENERAL CLAIM
```

## G. Source-hardening queue

Before either layer graduates, add authoritative or primary sources for:

1. developmental plasticity / critical periods across neural and endocrine systems;
2. allostasis and allostatic load with careful distinction between useful adaptation and pathology;
3. tissue remodeling / wound-healing persistence beyond illustrative scar language;
4. human longitudinal microbiome stability and perturbation/recovery;
5. household/social transmission of microbiota with strain-level evidence;
6. microbiome causal-inference methodology and confound control;
7. holobiont / hologenome debate from evolutionary-biology sources representing both supportive and critical positions;
8. randomized human trials for any claimed psychobiotic or microbiome-targeted therapeutic effect before therapeutic language is promoted.

## H. Standing anti-equations

```text
biological memory ≠ perfect recording
history-dependence ≠ determinism
epigenetics ≠ inherited trauma shorthand
intergenerational ≠ transgenerational
microbiome association ≠ microbiome causation
gut-brain axis ≠ one gut-to-brain cable
microbial metabolite effect ≠ microbe controls consciousness
physiological partnership ≠ shared personhood
host + microbiome interaction ≠ automatically one evolutionary individual
"natural" microbiota intervention ≠ established safe/effective treatment
```

> **Wild hypotheses are welcome. Convergence must be earned.**
