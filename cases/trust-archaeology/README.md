# Trust archaeology — the entrusted corpus

Status: **active research case / architectural extraction**

This case asks a narrow but deep question:

> What did human societies discover, across different legal and institutional traditions, about keeping a corpus, purpose, benefit, and authority coherent across changes in possession, operators, and time?

The case began from research into the history and future of trusts, but it is deliberately broader than modern trust doctrine and deliberately narrower than a legal implementation project.

## Why National Treasure owns the archaeology

The Static Collective already has executable ideas involving adopted roots, authority, warrants, receipts, constituted reality, and continuity. That makes historical trust structures unusually relevant — and unusually easy to overread.

National Treasure is the correct first home because it can preserve resemblance, lineage hypotheses, contradictions, and source families without forcing any of them into current system law.

## Working architectural hypothesis

A trust can be read as an old institutional machine for keeping several relations distinct:

```text
corpus      != custodian
custody     != ownership
ownership   != benefit
benefit     != authority
authority   != authorship
authority   != purpose
purpose     != current operator
```

The useful extraction is not “software should imitate trust law.” It is:

> **Durable systems may need purpose-bearing corpus identity plus succession-capable, accountable stewardship.**

That hypothesis is architectural. Its historical genealogy remains a research question. Its legal application, if any, belongs in a separate professional/legal process.

## Five veins to mine

1. **Corpus** — what remains the same thing when custody, storage, representation, or operator changes?
2. **Entrusted authority** — what powers were received, from whom, for what purpose, over what object, with what limits?
3. **Purpose** — what constrains otherwise mechanically possible exercises of power?
4. **Accounting** — what evidence must survive an exercise, refusal, delegation, failure, or succession?
5. **Succession** — how can stewardship change without silently resetting identity or purpose?

## Research lanes

The historical lane should test, not assume, relationships among:

- ancient custody, agency, inheritance, endowment, and fiduciary-like arrangements;
- Roman `fiducia` and `fideicommissum`;
- medieval and Germanic antecedents proposed in trust histories;
- Islamic `waqf` as a comparative institutional form rather than an assumed ancestor;
- English uses, Chancery, equitable obligation, and the Statute of Uses;
- emergence of the modern trust and divided legal/equitable interests;
- United States trust doctrine and later statutory forms;
- modern cross-border recognition, digital assets, directed administration, automation, and succession pressures.

Similarity is not transmission. Functional convergence is not genealogy. Each claimed bridge needs its own evidence.

## Safety boundary

This case is **not**:

- a declaration that the Static Collective or any repository is legally held in trust;
- a trust agreement, operating agreement, estate plan, tax position, asset-protection plan, or legal opinion;
- a claim that ancient or religious structures automatically control modern law;
- a claim that software receipts satisfy fiduciary accounting requirements;
- a claim that the words `trust`, `trustee`, `beneficiary`, or `corpus` should be imported into code merely because they are evocative.

Any eventual real-world legal structure must be evaluated separately with qualified human counsel or the user's trust consultant in the relevant jurisdiction.

## Current verdict

**Preserve aggressively; promote cautiously.**

There is enough architectural convergence to justify a serious research case and a downstream design vocabulary. There is not yet enough source-ingested historical work in this repository to claim a continuous genealogy, and there is intentionally no legal adoption in this slice.
