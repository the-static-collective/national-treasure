# Trust archaeology source map

Status: **baseline source intake; not exhaustive**

This file records source families used to ground the first architectural slice. It is a beginning, not the research report.

## Modern trust structure

### Cornell Legal Information Institute — Wex: Trust

URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/trust

Use: baseline modern U.S. description of trust as divided property rights / fiduciary relationship, with trustee holding/managing assets and beneficiary receiving beneficial enjoyment.

Caution: Wex is an explanatory legal reference, not a jurisdiction-specific governing instrument.

### Cornell Legal Information Institute — Wex: Trustee

URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/trustee

Use: baseline for fiduciary duties, separation of trust assets, recordkeeping, and successor-trustee continuity.

Caution: details vary by jurisdiction and trust instrument.

## English uses and trusts

### John Baker — *Baker and Milsom Sources of English Legal History: Private Law to 1750*, chapter 4, “Uses, wills and trusts”

URL: https://academic.oup.com/book/32441/chapter/268776508

Use: strong legal-history orientation to early uses, the Statute of Uses, subsequent equitable interests, and post-1535 trust recognition.

Caution: source access may be partial; ingest underlying primary materials separately before making fine-grained claims.

## Roman comparison

### David Johnston — *The Roman Law of Trusts*

URL: https://academic.oup.com/book/3436

Use: specialist treatment of `fideicommissum`, emphasizing that Roman law did not possess the common-law trust in the modern sense while developing a device capable of similar ends in succession.

Caution: similarity does not establish direct transmission into English trust doctrine.

## Waqf comparison

### Jeffrey A. Schoenblum — “The Role of Legal Doctrine in the Decline of the Islamic Waqf: A Comparison with the Trust”

URL: https://scholarship.law.vanderbilt.edu/faculty-publications/437/

Use: comparative analysis that explicitly warns against superficial equivalence between waqf and trust while examining overlapping wealth-management functions.

### Irina Gvelesiani — “The trust and the waqf (comparative analysis)”

URL: https://academic.oup.com/tandt/article-abstract/26/8-9/737/5918715

Use: recent comparative scholarship discussing similarities/differences and the debated Islamic-origin hypothesis.

### Mohammad Abdullah — “Waqf versus implied trust: a comparison of form and substance”

URL: https://academic.oup.com/tandt/article-abstract/22/3/336/2364550

Use: narrower comparison emphasizing specific structural affinities and differences rather than collapsing the institutions.
