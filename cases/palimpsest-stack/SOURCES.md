# Palimpsest stack sources

Status: **baseline source map; not an exhaustive bibliography**

The purpose of this file is to preserve enough provenance to re-open each lane without turning one web page into an authority for the entire case.

## Alphabetic lineage

### Metropolitan Museum of Art — alphabet origins

- Source: The Met, “Alphabet Origins: From Kipling to Sinai”
- Locator: https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2014/assyria-to-iberia/blog/posts/alphabet
- Use: West Semitic / Proto-Sinaitic background; Phoenician adoption and adaptation by Greek and Etruscan writers; acrophonic ancestry of letter names/forms.
- Boundary: museum synthesis, not a substitute for specialist palaeography.

### Oxford Handbook — Phoenician alphabet legacy

- Source: Madadh Richey, “The Alphabet and its Legacy,” in *The Oxford Handbook of the Phoenician and Punic Mediterranean* (2019)
- Locator: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34280/chapter-abstract/290618483
- Use: specialist overview of Phoenician standardization and Greek adaptation.

### UCL Digital Egypt — Greek writing

- Source: University College London, “Writing in Egypt: Greek”
- Locator: https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/writing/greek.html
- Use: concise material-culture orientation for early Greek alphabet forms and Phoenician similarity.

## Enoch / Metatron / Dee

### Posen Library — 3 Enoch

- Source: Posen Library, “3 Enoch”
- Locator: https://www.posenlibrary.com/entry/3-enoch
- Use: dates and frames *3 Enoch / Sefer Hekhalot*; preserves the Metatron–Enoch identification and notes that the title “3 Enoch” is scholarly rather than manuscript-native.
- Boundary: accessible scholarly edition/synthesis; use manuscript editions for text-critical claims.

## Overlapping-circle geometry

### Kha artifact analysis

- Source: Amelia Carolina Sparavigna and Mauro Maria Baldi, “Overlapping Circles Grid Drawn with Compass and Straightedge on an Egyptian Artifact of 14th Century BC” (2016)
- Locator: https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08536
- Use: evidence that an overlapping-circle grid appears on an Egyptian artifact far earlier than the modern “Flower of Life” name.
- Boundary: one artifact/study does not establish the later sacred-geometry doctrine, a worldwide transmission chain, or the age of the modern name.

### Modern-name trail

- Source: Drunvalo Melchizedek, *The Ancient Secret of the Flower of Life*, vol. 1 (1999/2000 editions)
- Use: a clearly modern popular source using and promoting the “Flower of Life” terminology and associated doctrine.
- Boundary: do not treat the title's “ancient” framing as evidence that the terminology itself is ancient.

## Escher

### M. C. Escher official site

- Source: M.C. Escher Foundation / official site, biography

## Francis Bacon / ciphers

### Bacon's biliteral method

- Source family: Francis Bacon, *De Augmentis Scientiarum*, Book VI; later English translations reproduce the method.
- Accessible locator preserving the relevant translated passage and facsimile discussion: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70119/pg70119-images.html
- Use: Bacon's actual two-class / five-position cipher construction.
- Boundary: the accessible volume is Elizabeth Wells Gallup's later Baconian work and therefore is **not** neutral evidence for her Shakespeare-decoding claims. Use it only to recover the quoted Bacon method and facsimile trail; evaluate Baconian authorship claims independently.

## Modern “Metatron's Cube” claim lane

No premodern primary source for the named thirteen-circle complete-connection figure was located in this pass.

That absence is preserved as an open research obligation, not converted into proof that no such source can exist.

Current web materials overwhelmingly present the figure inside modern sacred-geometry discourse. Until a dated earlier witness is produced, the repository treats:

```text
Metatron tradition
    !=
modern diagram named "Metatron's Cube"
```

The burden of proof is on the ancestry edge.
