# Attributable Transformation

Status: **Inference / incubating clue**

This page synthesizes relations exposed by independently owned National Treasure cases. It does not upgrade the evidence status of any source case and is not shared architectural law.

## Candidate compression

> **continuity ≠ preserved state; continuity = inspectable relation through change.**

A stronger formulation worth testing is:

> **Difference is not necessarily damage to continuity. Properly witnessed difference may be evidence that continuity occurred at all.**

The word **witnessed** is load-bearing. Difference by itself proves neither ancestry nor identity.

## 1. Attributable transformation

Several separate specimens now make final-state sameness look like the wrong continuity test:

- [`forkability-continuity`](../cases/forkability-continuity/) asks when divergent histories can preserve continuity through attributable ancestry, transformations, surviving differences, and conditions of re-entry;
- [`palimpsest-stack`](../cases/palimpsest-stack/) separates form, meaning, and name while locating continuity in witnessed transformations rather than superficial resemblance;
- [`genesis-continuity`](../cases/genesis-continuity/) independently treats division, differentiation, descendants, rupture, covenant, and return as continuity questions without claiming that biblical texts encode modern biology;
- [`hopi-backdoor-daughter-dna`](../cases/hopi-backdoor-daughter-dna/) tests descendant difference as a bounded route toward ancestral reconstruction while preserving the limits of genetic and documentary evidence;
- [`time-under-observation`](../cases/time-under-observation/) preserves recurrence inside irreversible change rather than treating a cycle as recreation of the prior world.

The shared pressure is narrower than any one domain:

```text
ancestor / prior state
        ↓
attributable transformation
        ↓
descendant / later state
        ↓
inspectable surviving relation
```

A changed descendant may remain continuous with an ancestor. A similar-looking object without an attributable road may not.

## 2. Residue can be navigational information

The Daughter case sharpens a useful possibility: surviving differences in descendants can sometimes help reconstruct the road toward an ancestral threshold.

Git history provides a mechanical analogue. Manuscript variants provide an imperfect historical analogue. Linguistic descendants can likewise preserve transformations that point backward even when the old form no longer survives intact.

The bounded claim is:

> **When ancestry is independently grounded, surviving differences may help reconstruct the path toward an ancestor or prior state.**

This is not `difference = ancestry`. The difference becomes navigational only inside an evidenced lineage.

### Cicada: the standing anti-overfit control

[`cicada-overfit`](../cases/cicada-overfit/) is essential here because its matched fixtures distinguish witnessed mixed descent from independent recurrence:

```text
A ---witnessed ancestry---\
                           > D
B ---witnessed ancestry---/

versus

P ---same visible feature--- Q
          no witnessed road
```

The first specimen has attributable incoming ancestry edges. The second preserves the resemblance while refusing the ancestry edge.

> **Resemblance may suggest a road. Only attributable transmission evidence may establish ancestry.**

That rule prevents `the descendant is a return address` from turning into resemblance-based genealogy. Mystery can motivate inspection; it cannot silently promote itself into verification or authority.

## 3. Thresholds may be formative

A boundary does not always act on a finished thing.

Some crossings merely relocate an already-distinguishable entity. Other thresholds may participate in making a descendant distinguishable at all. The current cases suggest several structurally different examples worth comparing:

- Beringian population formation and later dispersal in the Daughter research;
- branch creation as the beginning of independently continuing histories in forkability;
- division followed by descendants in Genesis;
- sound/form crossing into names, histories, and speaker ecologies in [`selador`](../cases/selador/).

The open research question is:

> **When does crossing a boundary preserve an already-formed identity, and when is the crossing itself part of the process that forms the descendant identity?**

No universal threshold primitive is claimed here.

## 4. Names have two arrows of time

The Selador and palimpsest cases expose an important asymmetry.

### Forward — generative naming

A name can become an attractor around which later meaning accumulates. A small resonant form may lawfully acquire descendants: stories, usages, speaker communities, conventions, project behavior, or an invented world.

The later growth does not need to have existed before the name.

### Backward — retrospective naming

A later label applied to earlier material has essentially no automatic authority to manufacture ancient ancestry. A compelling retrospective name can organize investigation, but it cannot make the earlier objects members of one historical lineage without independent evidence.

So:

```text
name → later descendants        can be generative
later name → invented ancestors cannot be evidentiary
```

Cicada supplies the same refusal boundary from another direction: recognition is not a witnessed road.

## 5. Return is not restoration

[`time-under-observation`](../cases/time-under-observation/) lands on **cycles nested inside irreversible change**. Forkability permits encounter and re-entry without requiring all branches to collapse into one history.

Together they suggest a durable distinction:

> **A lawful return revisits an attributable relation; it does not erase what happened since departure.**

A chorus can return changed. A branch can re-enter relation without pretending it never diverged. A reconstructed world can become navigable again without becoming the original occurrence.

That means these must remain distinguishable:

```text
recurrence ≠ reset
re-entry   ≠ uninterrupted presence
return     ≠ restoration
reconstruction ≠ original occurrence
```

Intervening residue belongs to the history.

## What would weaken this clue

The compression should lose force if materially different specimens repeatedly show that:

- final-state sameness is required for the relevant kind of continuity;
- alleged lineage cannot be reconstructed without resemblance-based inference;
- differences systematically destroy rather than preserve navigable ancestry;
- re-entry requires erasing or falsifying intervening history;
- the five distinctions only cohere because domain-specific meanings were flattened into vague metaphor.

Independent recurrence in Cicada is already a permanent negative specimen: visible similarity without a road does **not** count as continuity evidence.

## Promotion boundary

This clue is not ready for direct promotion into shared `patterns/` law.

Graduation would require materially independent positive **and adversarial** specimens showing that the compression remains useful without erasing local semantics. In particular, future review should continue asking:

- Can the relation be reconstructed without final-state sameness?
- Are ancestry and transformation edges attributable?
- Can rupture and failed re-entry remain representable?
- Can return preserve intervening residue?
- Does the distinction remain useful outside genealogy/history metaphors?

Until then:

> **Difference may witness continuity. Difference never gets to manufacture its own ancestry.**
