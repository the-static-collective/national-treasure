# Part I — Messiah as recomposition center

## 1. The carrier hypothesis

The New Testament does not merely attach isolated prooftexts to Jesus. Across several writers it presents Jesus in relation to multiple earlier scriptural identities, offices, patterns, and institutions.

A minimal graph:

~~~text
                     ADAM
                       │
ISRAEL / SON ──────────┤
                       │
MOSES / PROPHET ───────┤
                       │
DAVID / KING ──────────┤
                       │
SUFFERING SERVANT ─────┤
                       │
SON OF MAN ────────────┤
                       ▼
                     JESUS
                       ▲
                       │
TEMPLE ────────────────┤
                       │
PASSOVER ──────────────┤
                       │
JUBILEE / RELEASE ─────┤
                       │
NEW COVENANT ──────────┘
~~~

These earlier nodes are **not synonyms**.

~~~text
king != temple
Israel != Passover
Moses != Adam
servant != Son of Man
Jubilee != covenant
~~~

The New Testament's Christological move can therefore be modeled as:

> **The nodes do not become identical to one another. They become newly related through their asserted relation to Jesus.**

That makes Jesus, in the textual claim, not merely one more node but a **relation-generating particular**.

## 3. Hosea as provenance control

Hosea 11:1 is particularly useful because the earlier discourse is explicit:

~~~text
"When Israel was a child..."
~~~

The verse speaks retrospectively about Israel and the Exodus.

Matthew 2:15 nevertheless applies the line to Jesus.

A provenance-preserving representation therefore MUST NOT silently rewrite:

~~~text
Hosea 11:1
original referent = Jesus
~~~

A safer representation is:

~~~yaml
source:
  text: Hosea 11:1
  discourse_state: Israel / Exodus
  temporal_orientation: retrospective

later_relation:
  witness: Matthew 2:15
  asserted_relation: Jesus <-> Israel-pattern
  operation: fulfillment / recapitulation / typological relation
  source_state_preserved: true
~~~

This gives the packet a hostile control:

> **Can a later fulfillment relation be asserted strongly without falsifying the earlier discourse-state?**

If no, the model fails provenance pressure.

# Part II — Delayed recognition

## 4. John preserves occurrence-first / recognition-later

John 2:19–22 is an unusually clean specimen.

The narrative preserves:

~~~text
t1 — Jesus says a temple/body saying
t2 — hearers misunderstand / relation unresolved
t3 — resurrection
t4 — disciples remember
t5 — Scripture + saying become newly related in recognition
~~~

John 12:16 supplies a similar pattern: the disciples do not understand certain events at first; after Jesus is glorified, they remember that these things had been written concerning him.

The packet therefore distinguishes:

~~~text
RELATION CLAIMED TO EXIST
    !=
RELATION AVAILABLE TO RECEIVER
    !=
RELATION IN FOCUS
    !=
RELATION RECOGNIZED
    !=
RELATION RECORDED
~~~

Candidate extraction:

> **Later recognition need not be represented as retroactive prior knowledge.**

This is directly relevant to 3rdi-style occurrence / availability / focus / known-at distinctions, but the source does not derive historically from 3rdi and 3rdi does not gain authority from the resemblance.

# Part III — Jubilee before Luke

## 5. Leviticus: release as scheduled legal institution

Leviticus 25:8–17 presents Jubilee in calendrical/legal terms.

Minimal structure:

~~~text
fiftieth year
    ↓
10 Tishrei / Day of Atonement
    ↓
horn sounded
    ↓
deror / liberty proclaimed
    ↓
return to possession
    ↓
return to family
~~~

This remains the source-state against which later Jubilee-like transformations should be measured.

---

## 6. Isaiah 61: release becomes prophetic and agent-bearing

Isaiah 61 opens with a Spirit-bearing/anointed speaker who proclaims good news, release/liberty, and the favorable year of YHWH.

The transformation is already internal to the inherited corpus:

~~~text
Leviticus:
WHEN the appointed year comes,
proclaim release.

        ↓

Isaiah:
an ANOINTED / SPIRIT-BEARING AGENT
proclaims release
and the favorable year.
~~~

The lexical and conceptual relation between Isaiah 61 and Jubilee has a substantial scholarly history; the packet does not require that every detail of Isaiah 61 be reduced to Leviticus 25.

---

## 7. 11QMelchizedek: pre-Christian eschatological recomposition

11QMelchizedek (11Q13), a Second Temple Jewish text from Qumran, is a major control against the claim that Christianity invented the move from legal Jubilee to eschatological/agent-centered Jubilee.

Modern scholarship describes 11QMelchizedek as drawing together material from Leviticus, Deuteronomy, Isaiah and Psalms into an eschatological Jubilee interpretation in which Melchizedek functions as a heavenly liberating/judicial figure.

Useful scholarly controls:

- Logan Williams, **"Melchizedek, the Son of Man, and Eschatological Jubilee: The Sin-Forgiving Messiahs in 11QMelchizedek and Mark"**, Journal for the Study of the New Testament (2023):  
  https://doi.org/10.1177/0142064X231191176
- Simon J. Joseph, **"The Land Is Mine (Leviticus 25:23): Reimagining the Jubilee in the Context of the Palestinian-Israeli Conflict"** (2020), discussion of 11QMelchizedek as an eschatological Jubilee pesher:  
  https://doi.org/10.1177/0146107920958985

The bounded conclusion is:

~~~text
PRE-CHRISTIAN JEWISH INTERPRETATION
already permits:

Jubilee
    ↓
eschatological release
    ↓
scriptural recomposition
    ↓
agent-centered liberation
~~~

This makes Luke's move historically less isolated.

It does **not** establish direct Luke -> 11QMelchizedek literary dependence.

# Part IV — Luke's Jubilee seam

## 8. Luke 4 is already a recomposed quotation

Luke 4:18–19 is not a simple unmodified quotation of one source.

Scholarly analysis widely recognizes the passage as built principally from Isaiah 61:1–2 with material from Isaiah 58:6. The shared aphesis / release language is especially important.

Control:

- M. J. Obiorah, **"The spirit of the Lord God is upon me (Is 61:1): The use of Isaiah 61:1–2 in Luke 4:18–19"**, HTS Teologiese Studies 74.1 (2018):  
  https://hts.org.za/index.php/hts/article/view/5038/11923

Minimal textual shape:

~~~text
ISAIAH 61
anointing
good news
release
favorable year

        +

ISAIAH 58
oppressed sent in release

        ↓

LUKE 4
programmatic mission proclamation
~~~

The inheritance is therefore already being **composed** before verse 21 supplies the temporal hinge.

## 9. The crucial fact: "today" is not in the Isaiah quotation

Luke's reading reaches:

~~~text
"...to proclaim the favorable year of the Lord."
~~~

Then the narrative explicitly says:

~~~text
scroll closed
scroll returned
Jesus sits
eyes fixed on him
~~~

Only then comes the interpretive declaration:

~~~text
TODAY
this Scripture
has been fulfilled
in your hearing.
~~~

Therefore:

> **"TODAY" belongs to the interpretation / fulfillment declaration, not to the inherited Isaiah wording being quoted.**

That is one of the strongest receipts in this packet.

# Part V — Deconstruction of ΣΗΜΕΡΟΝ / TODAY

## 10. Lexical floor

The Greek word is:

~~~text
σήμερον
sēmeron
TODAY / THIS DAY
~~~

LSJ attests it as an ordinary Greek adverb meaning **today**, with usage going back to Homer and well before Christianity.

LSJ / Scaife ATLAS:

https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn%3Acite2%3Ascaife-viewer%3Adictionaries.v1%3Alsj-n93824/

The lexicon gives a probable derivation involving an old demonstrative stem ("this") related to ἡμέρα / day.

The minimal semantic floor is therefore:

~~~text
THIS DAY
~~~

Not:

~~~text
"Messianic age" by lexical definition
~~~

and not:

~~~text
"sign" or σημεῖον by etymology
~~~

The word is first of all **deictic**: it locates an utterance relative to a speaker's temporal position.

---

## 11. Luke 4:21 as a triple deictic lock

Greek:

~~~text
Σήμερον πεπλήρωται ἡ γραφὴ αὕτη ἐν τοῖς ὠσὶν ὑμῶν.
~~~

A deliberately wooden structural rendering:

~~~text
TODAY
has-been-fulfilled
THIS SCRIPTURE
in YOUR EARS.
~~~

The verse therefore carries at least three localizers:

~~~text
ΣΗΜΕΡΟΝ
TODAY
        → WHEN?

ἡ γραφὴ ΑὝΤΗ
THIS SCRIPTURE
        → WHICH SOURCE?

ἐν τοῖς ὠσὶν ὙΜΩΝ
IN YOUR EARS
        → AVAILABLE TO WHICH RECEIVERS?
~~~

Between them:

~~~text
ΠΕΠΛΗΡΩΤΑΙ
HAS BEEN FULFILLED
~~~

BibleHub's morphology identifies πεπλήρωται as perfect indicative middle/passive, third singular:

https://biblehub.com/strongs/luke/4-21.htm

This does not by itself settle every aspect of Greek perfect semantics. The safe observation is narrower:

> Luke does not place the fulfillment in an unspecified distant future. The clause presents fulfillment as obtaining **today** in the scene.

---

## 12. "In your ears" as receiver-local language

The phrase is literally built with ὦτα / ears.

The common English idiom "in your hearing" is sensible, but preserving the body-part helps expose the structure:

~~~text
source text
   ↓
read aloud
   ↓
sound
   ↓
ears
   ↓
receivers
~~~

A bounded inference is therefore:

> **The fulfillment claim is not floating in abstract time; Luke binds it to a concrete source, a concrete temporal index, and concrete hearers.**

It does **not** follow that the hearers' agreement causes the fulfillment. The Nazareth narrative shortly supplies resistance rather than consensus.

Thus:

~~~text
receiver exposure
    !=
receiver assent
~~~

---

## 13. "Today" as crossing coordinate, not duration

The packet's strongest temporal model is:

~~~text
before t₀:
promise / scriptural expectation
available as inherited text

        ↓

at t₀:
TODAY
crossing declared

        ↓

after t₀:
fulfilled relation claimed
as presently operative
~~~

Candidate law:

> **TODAY identifies the coordinate of the asserted crossing; it need not define the total duration of what has crossed.**

This prevents a silly reading in which fulfillment would last only for the hours of that Sabbath.

# Part VI — Luke's broader "today" motif

## 14. Ordinary word, patterned literary use

Luke also uses σήμερον in ordinary temporal statements. The lexeme itself therefore must not be promoted into a technical code word.

But several prominent uses cluster around salvation / fulfillment:

~~~text
Luke 2:11
TODAY a Savior is born

Luke 4:21
TODAY this Scripture is fulfilled

Luke 5:26
we have seen remarkable things TODAY

Luke 19:5
TODAY I must stay at your house

Luke 19:9
TODAY salvation has come to this house

Luke 23:43
TODAY you will be with me in paradise
~~~

NET Bible notes the distinctive Lukan pattern and cites major Luke scholarship (Bock; Marshall):

https://classic.net.bible.org/passage.php/passage.php?passage=Luk+2%3A11%3B4%3A21%3B5%3A26%3B19%3A5%2C9

A recent peer-reviewed article also explicitly treats the Lukan "today" motif as a major Christological/salvific structure:

Peter Manwiller, **"The Son of Man and the 'Today' of Salvation: A Lukan Christology"**, Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society 69.2 (2026):

https://openurl.ebsco.com/contentitem/gcd%3A195647007

Bounded extraction:

> **Luke repeatedly uses literal "today" statements at moments where salvation, fulfillment, presence, healing, or promised benefit becomes narratively immediate.**

The literary pattern is stronger than the lexical claim.

# Part VII — The carrier turns WHEN into WHO

## 15. Jubilee's center of gravity moves

The historical/scriptural sequence can now be stated more carefully:

~~~text
LEVITICUS 25

WHEN the Jubilee year arrives
    ↓
proclaim release

        ↓

ISAIAH 61

an ANOINTED AGENT
    ↓
proclaims release
and the favorable year

        ↓

SECOND TEMPLE INTERPRETATION

Jubilee can be read
eschatologically and agent-centrically

        ↓

LUKE 4

Jesus reads the release / favorable-year text

        ↓

scroll closes

        ↓

"TODAY"
~~~

This produces the key relational transformation:

~~~text
Jubilee's older question:
WHEN is release?

Luke's narrated answer:
TODAY.

Why TODAY?

Because WHO is here?
~~~

This is an **interpretive inference**, not a lexical definition.

The strongest safe form is:

> **Luke presents Jesus' presence as the reason the inherited release proclamation can be indexed as fulfilled "today."**

# Kill conditions

Narrow or kill this packet's stronger inferences if:

1. today is treated as though its dictionary meaning were "Messianic age" rather than ordinary temporal deixis;
2. Luke 4 is used to assert that the Nazareth Sabbath was historically a Levitical Jubilee year without independent evidence;
3. 11QMelchizedek is represented as a direct literary source for Luke without a demonstrated transmission relation;
4. Hosea's Israel/Exodus discourse-state is erased in order to make Matthew's later relation easier;
5. distinct Christological carrier-lines are collapsed as though they had always been synonyms;
6. "carrier" becomes an excuse to ignore disagreement about fulfillment;
7. later Christian claims are silently presented as neutral historical-critical conclusions;
8. the Suno output is allowed to function as evidence rather than creative reception;
9. the Static Collective analogy begins manufacturing authority for either the biblical interpretation or the project architecture.

# Smallest pressure test

Given any asserted fulfillment/recomposition relation, attempt:

~~~text
1. Preserve the earlier source-state exactly enough to disagree with the later reading.
2. Record the later occurrence separately.
3. Record who recognizes/asserts the relation and when.
4. Type the operation: fulfillment, typology, recapitulation, office transfer, institution-person relation, etc.
5. Preserve receiver-local availability separately from assent.
6. Attach competing readings.
7. State nonclaims.
8. Ask whether removing the carrier destroys the new cross-relations among earlier nodes.
~~~

Hostile question:

> **Can a reader traverse backward through every hinge, recover the earlier source-state, understand why the later community asserted fulfillment, and still reject that assertion without the graph breaking?**

If yes, the representation may preserve inheritance without captivity.
