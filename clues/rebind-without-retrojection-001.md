## Why this clue exists

Recent work independently exposed two directions of the same identity problem:

1. A continuing logical address or referent can remain useful while its exact carrier/body changes.
2. An exact historical consequence still belongs to the exact body/carrier/event that bore it.

These are not competing identity strategies. They solve different problems.

## Four-way non-collapse


## Fibered identity model

Let

```math
\pi : B \to L
```

map exact embodiments `b in B` to a continuing logical identity `L`.

Then

```math
\pi^{-1}(L)
```

is the fiber of exact bodies/carriers that have lawfully borne that continuing identity.

An occurrence `o` resolves the continuing identity through an occurrence-local section:

```math
s_o(L)=b_o, \qquad b_o\in\pi^{-1}(L).
```

For a live organ:

```text
s_O17(ALEX) = ALEX@abc123
s_O18(ALEX) = ALEX@987xyz
```

Both statements preserve logical continuity while refusing body collapse.

If receipt `R1` was produced in `O17`, then:

```text
producer(R1) = ALEX@abc123
```

A later live resolution to `ALEX@987xyz` does not rewrite that historical producer relation.

## Target-relative quotient gate

For a declared target `T`, define:

```math
b_1 \sim_T b_2 \iff T(b_1)=T(b_2).
```

A body distinction may be omitted only when the target is invariant across that distinction.

Examples:

```text
T_owner:
    ALEX@A ~ ALEX@B
    output: owner = ALEX

T_replay:
    ALEX@A !~ ALEX@B
    exact body required

T_derivation:
    ALEX@A !~ ALEX@B
    exact body required

T_historical_attribution:
    ALEX@A !~ ALEX@B
    exact body required
```

Thus abstraction is lawful only relative to a declared question. A coarse projection does not destroy the need for exact provenance elsewhere.
