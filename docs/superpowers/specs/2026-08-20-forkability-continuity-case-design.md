# National Treasure — Forkability Continuity Case Design

**Status:** Approved research slice
**Date:** 2026-08-20

## Purpose

Preserve the newly revealed forkability thread as a claim-addressable National Treasure case without turning an architectural analogy into historical, technical, or social law.

## Research question

> **When does allowing divergence preserve more continuity than enforcing synchronization?**

## Evidence families

Keep four families independent:

1. Git divergence / explicit merge mechanics;
2. local-first and CRDT asynchronous replication;
3. manuscript genealogy / CBGM ancestry restraints;
4. Upper Room human-social continuity without captivity.

Cross-family resemblance may support an inference only after each family keeps its own provenance and limits.

## Required counterevidence

The case must prominently preserve:

- merge conflicts;
- abandoned branches;
- duplicated work;
- decentralized authorization/revocation difficulty;
- data convergence without semantic agreement;
- permanent schism;
- false ancestry inferred from resemblance.

## Claim boundary

Allowed candidate inference:

> A system can preserve continuity across divergence when ancestry, transformations, surviving differences, and conditions of re-entry remain attributable.

Rejected unconditional form:

> branching preserves continuity.

## Graduation boundary

`Re-entry Topology` remains speculation/incubation only. No shared primitive or downstream implementation is authorized by this research case.
